# The Lighthouse That Refused To Go Dark

For thirty-one years my grandfather kept the light on Rell Point, and in all
that time he never once missed a night.

The mainland decided the lighthouse was obsolete. A committee sent a letter
saying automation would take over in spring, and that his services were no
longer required. He read it twice, folded it in half, and went up the stairs to
polish the lens anyway.

That March a storm came in faster than the forecast, and the new automated
system failed within the hour. The relay tripped, the backup did not engage,
and the whole point went black. Somewhere out past the shoals a fishing boat
was running for harbour with no idea where the rocks were.

My grandfather climbed the tower in the dark with a hurricane lamp and a mirror
he had taken off his own wall. He stood at the window for six hours turning
that lamp by hand, one slow sweep after another, because that was the rhythm
the boats knew.

The boat made harbour at dawn. The captain walked up the hill, looked at the
old man still holding the lamp, and did not say anything at all for a while.

They rebuilt the automation that summer. They also, quietly, put his name on a
brass plate at the base of the tower. He never mentioned it to us. We found it
years later, on a walk, after he was gone.
